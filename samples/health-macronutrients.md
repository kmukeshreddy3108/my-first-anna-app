# Macronutrients and micronutrients

Nutrition lessons often divide nutrients into macronutrients and
micronutrients. Macronutrients are needed in comparatively large amounts and
include carbohydrates, proteins, and fats. Carbohydrate and protein provide
about 4 kilocalories per gram, while fat provides about 9 kilocalories per
gram. Water is also required in large amounts, although it does not provide
energy. Energy needs vary with age, body size, activity, health, and goals.

Carbohydrates are broken down into sugars and can supply glucose for cells.
Fiber is a carbohydrate that is not fully digested; it supports bowel
function and may help regulate blood glucose and cholesterol. Protein is made
of amino acids. It supports muscles, enzymes, hormones, transport proteins,
and immune structures. Dietary protein comes from beans, lentils, eggs,
fish, dairy, meat, nuts, and seeds.

Fats provide concentrated energy and help absorb vitamins A, D, E, and K.
Unsaturated fats in foods such as olive oil, nuts, seeds, and fish are often
recommended in place of trans fats. Saturated fat guidance depends on the
overall diet and individual health. Nutrition labels should be read in context:
percent daily values are reference points, not personalized prescriptions.

Micronutrients include vitamins and minerals. Iron helps carry oxygen, calcium
supports bones and muscle function, and vitamin C supports collagen formation
and improves absorption of plant iron. Vitamin D contributes to bone health
and immune function. A deficiency can cause problems, but more is not always
better; excessive supplements can be harmful. Variety improves the chance of
meeting needs.

A practical plate can include vegetables or fruit, a protein source, a
whole-grain or starchy food, and a source of unsaturated fat. For an adult
eating 2,000 kcal, a label might use 50% as a daily value for a nutrient, but
that percentage is only a comparison tool. People with allergies, medical
conditions, or special dietary needs should use advice from a qualified
professional rather than applying a generic number.
